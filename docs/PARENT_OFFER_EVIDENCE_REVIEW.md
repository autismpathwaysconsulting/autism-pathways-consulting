# APC Parent Offers — International Evidence and Structure Review

Status: Internal evidence review, September 2026
Purpose: test whether APC's Free Call, RM350 One-Concern Parent Session and RM1,800 Home Support Programme are structurally defensible, and identify improvements without implying therapy or clinical treatment.

## Executive conclusion

There is no research basis for calling one exact commercial package the universally “best” structure. The strongest cross-program pattern is instead:

**teach/plan → caregiver practice in everyday routines → feedback/coaching → review/adjustment → maintenance/follow-up.**

APC's current ladder is directionally sound if the offers remain clearly differentiated:

- Free Call = fit/routing
- RM350 = one focused educational consultation and action plan
- RM1,800 = first implementation cycle over several weeks

The RM1,800 programme is closer to the implementation logic used in evaluated caregiver programmes than a one-off consultation, but it is lighter/shorter than many structured research programmes. It should therefore be marketed as a practical support cycle, not as a comprehensive evidence-based autism intervention.

## International reference models

### WHO Caregiver Skills Training (CST)
WHO's CST package for caregivers of children with developmental delays/disabilities uses:
- 9 group sessions,
- 3 individual home visits,
- everyday play/home routines as practice contexts,
- goal-setting,
- demonstration/coaching,
- review of home practice.

Sources:
- https://www.who.int/teams/mental-health-and-substance-use/treatment-care/who-caregivers-skills-training-for-families-of-children-with-developmental-delays-and-disorders
- https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240048973

Relevance to APC:
- supports repeated contact and real-life practice,
- supports goal review rather than one-off information,
- does NOT mean APC is delivering WHO CST.

### RUBI parent training trial
Bearss et al. (JAMA, 2015) compared structured parent training with parent education for autistic children with disruptive behaviour.

The parent-training arm included:
- 11 core sessions,
- up to 2 optional sessions,
- 1 home visit during the active phase,
- up to 6 parent-child coaching sessions over 16 weeks,
- a further home visit and 2 telephone booster sessions between weeks 16 and 24.

Source:
https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2275445

Relevance to APC:
- illustrates the importance of active practice/coaching and booster contact,
- shows that maintenance support can extend beyond the initial active phase,
- should not be used to imply APC is delivering RUBI or behaviour therapy.

### Parent-implemented intervention meta-analysis
A meta-analysis of parent-implemented interventions for autistic children reported moderate average benefits across multiple domains, while lower-risk-of-bias studies yielded somewhat smaller estimates.

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35996037/

Relevance to APC:
- supports caregiver implementation as a meaningful mechanism,
- does not validate APC's exact package, dose or price,
- reinforces the need to avoid guaranteed child-outcome claims.

### Recent systematic reviews
Recent reviews of parent-mediated interventions continue to show positive but variable effects across parent-child interaction, responsiveness, social communication and other outcomes, with effect size and certainty depending on intervention/model and study quality.

Examples:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40120018/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40358871/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38664754/

Implication:
APC should call its structure **evidence-informed**, not “proven” or “evidence-based treatment,” unless the exact APC programme itself is evaluated.

## Review of APC offer ladder

### 1. Free 15-Minute First Step Call

Evidence role:
This is not an intervention. Its job is triage/routing and informed purchasing.

Recommended structure:
- minimal pre-booking context,
- clarify need/scope,
- decide RM350 vs RM1,800 vs referral/no paid service,
- no personalised strategy work.

Verdict: **KEEP.**

Strengthening needed:
- require child age,
- require 1–2 sentence decision question,
- promise a routing answer, not a strategy answer.

### 2. RM350 One-Concern Parent Session — 45 minutes

Evidence role:
A single 45-minute consultation should not be represented as sufficient to create durable behaviour/developmental change.

It can still be a legitimate low-intensity product when the objective is:
- understand one focused pattern,
- identify one working hypothesis,
- choose 1–2 practical changes,
- define what to observe,
- identify when referral/greater support is required.

Recommended additions:
- structured pre-session intake,
- one-page APC One-Concern Action Note,
- one clarification message/email within 7 days (optional policy decision),
- explicit scope and referral threshold.

Verdict: **KEEP, but sell it as decision support / action planning, not an intervention programme.**

### 3. RM1,800 Home Support Programme — 4 x 60 minutes over 6–8 weeks

Evidence role:
Repeated contact with practice and feedback is more consistent with international caregiver-training evidence than one-off advice.

However, four sessions over 6–8 weeks is lighter than many evaluated caregiver programmes. That is not automatically a problem because APC is not claiming to replicate them.

Recommended positioning:

**A first implementation cycle:**
- understand the pattern,
- choose priority support,
- implement,
- review real-world response,
- adjust,
- consolidate/maintain.

Recommended cadence:
- Week 0: baseline/intake
- Week 1: Session 1 — Understand + first plan
- Week 3: Session 2 — Review + adjust
- Week 5: Session 3 — Build/generalise
- Week 7: Session 4 — Consolidate + maintenance plan
- Around Week 11: short 30-day maintenance check

The exact spacing can flex around family implementation needs.

Verdict: **KEEP and strengthen.**

## Should follow-up be quarterly or yearly?

International caregiver-training evidence does not support quarterly or annual meetings as the main cadence for initial implementation of a new plan. Active programmes generally use repeated sessions close enough together for practice and feedback.

Quarterly/annual review makes more sense after an active implementation phase.

Recommended APC maintenance architecture:

### Included
**30-day maintenance check** after the 4-session Home Support cycle.
Purpose:
- check whether the agreed support is still being used,
- identify drift/barriers,
- decide whether the family can continue independently or needs further support/referral.

Keep this brief so the RM1,800 economics remain sustainable.

### Optional later review
**3-month Progress Review**
Potentially a paid 45-minute existing-client review, using the RM350 session or a future named maintenance product only if demand justifies it.

Use for:
- new difficulties with the existing plan,
- generalisation to a new routine,
- school/home consistency,
- developmental/context change.

### Quarterly thereafter
Only when there is a meaningful reason to review. Do not create recurring appointments simply to manufacture retention.

### Annual review
Potentially useful as a broad annual reset when:
- school year changes,
- adolescence/puberty changes support needs,
- family priorities change,
- a major transition is approaching.

Annual-only contact is too sparse for active troubleshooting or implementing a new support plan.

## Recommended final parent ladder

### Free 15-Min First Step Call
Question answered:
**“Is APC the right route, and what should I book?”**

### RM350 One-Concern Parent Session
Question answered:
**“What may be contributing to this one repeated situation, and what should I test next?”**

Deliverable:
- one-page Action Note,
- clear next observation/review threshold.

### RM1,800 Home Support Programme
Question answered:
**“How do we implement, review and adjust support over several weeks?”**

Deliverables:
- baseline/priority summary,
- implementation plan,
- session review notes,
- maintenance plan,
- short 30-day check.

### Optional 3-month / transition-triggered review
Do not add publicly until the first-cycle model is operating reliably and demand is observed.

## What would make the structure stronger without creating clinical overreach

1. Make goals observable and family-relevant.
2. Keep implementation steps small enough to use between sessions.
3. Review whether the parent actually implemented the agreed change before assuming the strategy failed.
4. Revise working hypotheses when evidence does not fit.
5. Measure parent implementation/feasibility before claiming child outcome effects.
6. Document maintenance and referral thresholds.
7. Coordinate questions for existing professionals rather than replacing them.
8. Avoid unnecessary complexity and unlimited between-session messaging.

## Claim language

Safe:
- evidence-informed structure,
- structured parent education and implementation support,
- practical support planning,
- review and adjustment over time,
- helping parents apply agreed support in daily routines.

Avoid:
- evidence-based treatment (for APC itself),
- clinically proven APC programme,
- treatment plan,
- therapeutic dose,
- guaranteed developmental/behavioural improvement,
- claims that APC replaces OT/SLT/psychology/medical care.

## Commercial conclusion

Do not replace the RM350/RM1,800 ladder before testing the strengthened version.

The strongest next experiment is:
1. improve intake and deliverables,
2. add the 30-day maintenance check to Home Support,
3. track completion, parent implementation and reasons for needing further support,
4. observe whether families naturally request 3-month/quarterly reviews,
5. only then decide whether APC needs a recurring maintenance product.
