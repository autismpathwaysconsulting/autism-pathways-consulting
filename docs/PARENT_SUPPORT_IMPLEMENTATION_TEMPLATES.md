# APC Parent Support — Implementation Templates

Status: Internal working templates. Keep language educational/consultative, not diagnostic or therapeutic.

## A. RM350 One-Concern Parent Session — Pre-Session Intake

### Parent goal
What is the one repeated situation you most want help making sense of?

### Current pattern
- When does it usually happen?
- What tends to happen just before?
- What does your child do during the situation?
- What happens immediately after?
- What have you already tried?
- What seems to make it easier or harder?

### Current professional support
List only what is relevant to this concern.

### Parent decision question
By the end of the session, what would you most like to understand or decide?

## B. APC One-Concern Action Note

**Parent:** [name]
**Date:** [date]
**Concern:** [one focused concern]

### 1. What we noticed
Brief factual pattern from parent report. Separate observations from interpretation.

### 2. Working explanation
Use cautious wording:

> “A working possibility is…”
> “This may be influenced by…”
> “We do not yet know whether…”

Possible educational/consultative lenses where relevant:
- communication demand,
- regulation/arousal,
- predictability/routine,
- sensory/environmental load,
- task/learning demand,
- adult response,
- participation/independence.

Do not write a diagnosis or clinical formulation.

### 3. Priority for now
One priority only.

### 4. What to try
Maximum 1–2 practical changes.

For each:
- What will the adult do?
- When?
- What should stay consistent?
- What should the parent avoid changing at the same time?

### 5. What to observe
Choose a small number of observable indicators.
Examples:
- frequency/context,
- time needed to settle/transition,
- amount of adult prompting,
- whether the child initiates/participates,
- parent feasibility.

### 6. When to review
Specify a practical review point rather than “keep trying indefinitely.”

### 7. When to stop / refer / coordinate
Note safety concerns, medical/clinical questions or situations where another professional should be involved.

### Scope note
This Action Note records parent education and practical support planning. It is not a diagnosis, assessment, treatment plan or substitute for regulated clinical care.

## C. RM1,800 Home Support Programme — Baseline

### Family priority 1
What would make daily life meaningfully easier if it improved?

### Optional priority 2
Only include a second priority if it is realistically connected to the same implementation cycle.

### Current pattern
For each priority:
- setting/routine,
- what happens before,
- what happens during,
- what happens after,
- current adult response,
- what has been tried,
- what appears to help,
- what appears to worsen the situation.

### Starting indicators
Use simple baseline indicators that a parent can realistically observe.
Avoid burdensome daily data collection unless necessary.

### Existing supports
Relevant school/professional/family supports.

### Family constraints
Time, caregiver capacity, sibling needs, work schedule, helper involvement, living environment, cultural/family expectations.

### Meaningful change
Complete:

> “By the end of this first support cycle, we would like daily life to feel different because…”

## D. Session 1 — Understand + First Plan

### What seems most important now?
Separate:
- verified facts,
- parent report,
- working hypotheses,
- unknowns.

### First implementation target
One manageable change.

### Adult action
Exactly what the adult will do.

### Context
When/where it will be used.

### Minimum observation
What information will tell us whether to continue, change or stop?

### Feasibility check
Parent rates plan feasibility 0–10.
If below 7, simplify before ending the session.

---

## E. Between-Session Check

Keep this lightweight.

1. Were you able to try the agreed change?
   - Yes, most opportunities
   - Sometimes
   - Rarely/not yet

2. Where did you try it?

3. What happened?

4. What made it easier/harder to use?

5. Is there anything new involving safety or a major change in circumstances?

Important interpretation rule:
If implementation was low, do not conclude the strategy failed. First solve feasibility/implementation barriers.

---

## F. Session 2 — Review + Adjust

### Implementation first
- Was the plan actually used?
- Was it used as intended?
- Was it realistic for the family?

### Response
- what changed,
- what did not,
- unexpected effects,
- context differences.

### Decision
Choose one:
- continue as is,
- simplify,
- adjust,
- change environmental support,
- change adult response,
- revise the working hypothesis,
- refer/coordinate.

---

## G. Session 3 — Build / Generalise

Only extend once the first plan is reasonably workable.

Potential aims:
- use in another routine,
- involve another caregiver/helper,
- increase consistency,
- fade unnecessary prompting,
- prepare school questions,
- support generalisation without adding too many demands.

Document what must remain stable and what can now flex.

---

## H. Session 4 — Consolidate + Maintenance

### What the family learned
Not just “what strategy worked”, but:
- what pattern they now recognise,
- what their child seems to need,
- what adult responses are most useful,
- what is still uncertain.

### Continue
What should remain in place?

### Stop
What is no longer useful / is creating unnecessary burden?

### Early warning signs
What tells the family the plan is drifting or circumstances have changed?

### Self-review question
Before booking more help, what can the family check themselves?

### Escalation / referral threshold
When should the family return to APC vs contact another professional?

---

## I. 30-Day Maintenance Check

Recommended as a short included check after the first 4-session cycle.

Questions:
1. Which parts of the plan are still being used?
2. What has become easier?
3. What has drifted or become difficult again?
4. Has the context changed?
5. Does the family need:
   - no further support,
   - one focused RM350 review,
   - another implementation cycle,
   - coordination/referral to another professional?

Keep this check brief. It is a maintenance decision point, not an extra full programme session.

---

## J. Optional 3-Month Progress Review

Do not include automatically for every family.

Use when:
- the original plan needs adjustment,
- a new routine/context has appeared,
- school/home consistency needs review,
- there is a developmental/transition change,
- the family wants a focused progress check.

Potential commercial route:
Use the RM350 One-Concern Parent Session for one focused review until sufficient demand exists to justify a separate maintenance product.

---

## K. Data/claim discipline

For all parent documentation:
- distinguish facts from hypotheses,
- state source of information,
- avoid diagnostic labels not already established by an appropriate professional,
- measure implementation/feasibility before claiming strategy failure,
- avoid promising child outcomes,
- keep sensitive data to the minimum needed,
- recommend regulated professionals when the question exceeds APC scope.
