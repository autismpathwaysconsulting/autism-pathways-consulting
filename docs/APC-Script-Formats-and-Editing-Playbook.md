## Shared script architecture

Clear topic title + specific question -> recognisable situation -> information gap -> developing explanation -> visible support -> direct payoff -> purposeful ending.

1. Name a topic viewers can identify quickly.
2. Ask one question the actual script answers.
3. Begin the spoken content with something concrete: an object, an action, a parent situation or a directly relevant finding.
4. Make the missing information clear without inventing secrecy or fear.
5. Develop one explanation. Each beat should resolve something and make the next beat relevant.
6. Use examples, demonstrations or checked evidence to support the explanation.
7. Answer the title question explicitly.
8. Give a useful implication or manageable action, appropriate to the episode's purpose.
9. Finish with one relevant invitation, not a stack of unrelated engagement requests.

### Writing features to preserve

- Concrete nouns and observable actions before abstract terms.
- Short spoken sentences, with occasional short emphasis lines when they carry meaning.
- Clear links between sentences, rather than a disconnected list of tips.
- Parent relevance early enough that the viewer understands why to stay.
- A clear shift from uncertainty to understanding or from concern to an available next step.
- Evidence with its actual population, setting, date and limitations.
- Warm, specific language without blaming parents or stereotyping autistic people.
- A purposeful ending that returns to the original question.

### Features that are optional

- "Can I tell you something?" is a voice choice, not the substance of the hook.
- A statistic is useful when immediately relevant; it is not mandatory and need not open the video.
- A practical list is appropriate for some episodes, not every story.
- English/Malay caption pairs are an option observed in the PopMart reference, not a universal requirement.
- Personal experience may be used only when genuinely supplied or confirmed by CJ.
- Reference duration is descriptive. The longer reference videos do not automatically override current APC duration limits.

## Format A: Discovery and meaning

Use when a familiar object, public story or cultural reference has a checked autism-related connection.

- Title: [Familiar subject] & Autism
- Question: What is the connection?
- Opening: Identify something the viewer has seen or experienced.
- Curiosity: Point to a specific lesser-known detail.
- Development: Explain the connection in two or three linked stages.
- Support: Relevant source material or clearly labelled context.
- Payoff: Return to the familiar subject with a new, supported understanding.
- Ending: A meaningful reflection or relevant question.

Avoid unsupported origin stories, romanticised stereotypes and interpreting later campaign participation as proof of original artistic intent.

## Format B: Recognisable difficulty and practical demonstration

Use when the viewer needs to understand a specific everyday difficulty and see a manageable response.

- Title: Autism & [Specific experience]
- Question: Why does [observable situation] happen?
- Opening: Show or describe the situation.
- Tension: Identify what makes it confusing or hard.
- Explanation: Describe one possible contributing demand without making it the universal cause.
- Support: A concrete example and appropriately scoped evidence.
- Payoff: Show the suggested response and what it looks like in that same situation.
- Ending: One specific action to try or save.

Keep the same example through the explanation and demonstration. Do not claim diagnosis from one behaviour or guarantee outcomes.

## Format C: Parent information gap and navigation

Use when parents need clear, locally relevant information about services, costs or support.

- Title: [Specific parent issue] in [Location]
- Question: What does a parent need to know?
- Opening: Identify the parent and the decision or moment they face.
- Gap: State the missing practical information without exaggerated fear.
- Reveal: Provide checked, dated facts, figures or options.
- Meaning: Explain the implication for a realistic household or decision.
- Help: Show available routes, eligibility and limitations.
- Payoff: A concrete next step with enough detail to act on.
- Ending: A specific reason to save or share.

Do not treat advertised rates as universal costs or broad benefits as automatic entitlements. Personal observations are not comparative evidence about which families do best.

## Caption structure

1. Topic and question, or a concrete first line that establishes relevance.
2. Answer or useful implication near the beginning.
3. Additional explanation or a short, usable sequence.
4. Relevant qualifications and evidence links.
5. One topic-specific invitation.
6. Optional faithful Malay adaptation.

Captions must be understandable independently. They can expand the video, but should not silently change what the video claims. Avoid mandatory full-script duplication and mandatory decorative punctuation between every sentence.

## Accuracy lesson from the core-strength reference

The checked 2024 review included 37 studies: nine quantitatively synthesised and 28 qualitatively analysed. It concerned exercise interventions broadly. It does not establish that weak core muscles explain all the listed school difficulties or that one brief plank improves handwriting.

| Production clarity | 1 | Actual export reviewed for readable visuals, accurate captions and intelligible audio |
| Total | 10 | At least 9 with all critical gates passed |

A false claim, unverified citation, scope breach, missing promised payoff, or unresolved substantive duplicate blocks readiness regardless of the total. Unknown dimensions remain unscored, not zero and not assumed passes. A partial visual review receives a partial-review rating only. Existing Content OS evidence, red-team and full-export review gates still apply; this rubric is supplemental and does not manufacture their records.

### Preventing repeated episodes

Compare across ideas, drafts, recorded, published and archived episodes. Use the combination of parent situation + explanation/mechanism + practical action. A different title or household prop around the same reasoning and action is not automatically a distinct episode.

- DISTINCT: a materially different useful question or payoff, with shared background acknowledged.
- INTENTIONAL_VARIANT: same lesson deliberately testing a different opening or presentation. Link it to the original and declare the tested change.
- NEEDS_REVISION: same lesson presented as new without additional practical value.
- UNKNOWN: missing scripts or incomplete catalog prevents clearance.

For example, moving from a three-step kitchen task to a three-step school-bag task while retaining the same explanation and NOW/NEXT action is likely a variant. A sequel must answer a new question with a different useful payoff, not just repeat the checklist. This is a comparison example, not a newly cleared clinical recommendation.

The Episode Studio prompt now includes up to 30 other loaded records, including archived records, with script excerpts capped at 800 characters. It discloses the count and truncation. This assists editorial comparison; it is not an automated semantic detector or a complete-history guarantee. Fetch omitted records and full scripts before issuing complete clearance.
