# APC School / Educator PD — Decision-Maker Meeting & Demo

Use for a 15–20 minute first meeting. Do not teach proprietary Hanen content during the sales demo.

## Meeting goal

By the end of the meeting, the buyer should understand:
1. the problem APC is trying to solve,
2. why information-only training may not be enough,
3. the difference between Hanen early-childhood programmes and APC autism-specific support,
4. what a small pilot could look like,
5. what APC will and will not claim.

## 1. Opening — 60 seconds

“Thank you for meeting with me. Before I recommend any programme, I want to understand what your team is trying to handle better. APC’s school work is built around implementation. The question is not simply whether staff have heard a strategy before, but whether they can use it consistently in the situations that matter.”

Then ask:

“What is one recurring situation you would most like your educators to handle more confidently or consistently?”

## 2. Problem — 2 minutes

Use one synthetic example:

“Imagine a preschool group activity where one child rarely participates. The educator knows they should support communication, so they ask more questions. The child responds less. The team is caring and has knowledge, but the difficulty is translating that knowledge into the right interaction in that moment.”

Explain:
- training may increase knowledge,
- implementation requires practice and feedback,
- different problems need different routes.

Do not claim this exact example is a client case unless it actually is and permission exists.

## 3. Evidence — 2 minutes

Use cautious language:

“Teacher professional-development research gives us a reason to take implementation seriously. For example, the Kraft, Blazar and Hogan meta-analysis of 60 causal studies found sizeable effects of teacher coaching on instructional practice. This does not mean every coaching programme works equally well, but it supports moving beyond passive information delivery.”

Malaysia context:

“Recent Malaysian preschool research has also identified gaps in autism knowledge, attitudes and self-efficacy and recommended more targeted professional development. I treat that as evidence of a capability problem, not proof that APC is automatically the solution. That is why I want to start with a small, measurable pilot.”

## 4. APC model — 2 minutes

Show the sequence:

**Identify → Learn → Practise → Implement → Review**

Explain:
- identify the team’s actual problem,
- choose the right structured programme or APC consultation,
- practise/application during learning,
- staff use agreed strategies in context,
- review barriers and next steps.

## 5. Two service lanes — 3 minutes

### Lane A: Licensed Hanen early-childhood programmes

Use only high-level descriptions:
- Teacher Talk™ Training Series
- Learning Language and Loving It™
- ABC and Beyond™

Say explicitly:
“These are early-childhood educator programmes. They are not autism-specific programmes.”

### Lane B: APC autism-specific implementation support

Examples:
- inclusion and participation,
- regulation and overwhelm,
- transitions/routines,
- prompting and independence,
- team consistency,
- parent-school collaboration.

Say:
“This is APC’s own consultation/professional-learning work. It is separate from Hanen curriculum.”

## 6. Five-minute implementation demo

Do NOT show Hanen slides, videos or member-only handouts.

### Synthetic scenario

**Current situation**
A team says: “We keep prompting the learner through every step of a classroom routine. The routine gets completed, but independence is not increasing.”

### Step 1 — Define the observable problem
Not “the learner is dependent.”

Instead:
“Adults currently provide verbal or physical prompting at most steps of the routine.”

### Step 2 — Define the desired practice change
“Staff identify which steps genuinely require support and where they can pause before prompting.”

### Step 3 — Implementation plan
For one routine:
- define the sequence,
- identify current prompt level,
- agree where to wait,
- agree what counts as independent initiation,
- keep adult responses consistent.

### Step 4 — Review question
After one week:
- Did staff use the agreed approach?
- At which steps?
- What blocked implementation?
- Did the learner begin any steps with less support?

### Step 5 — Decision
- continue,
- simplify,
- adjust environment,
- coach staff further,
- or decide the original assumption was wrong.

Finish:
“This is the level at which I want professional development to become useful. Not only ‘did staff enjoy the workshop?’ but ‘what did they actually use, and what happened when they tried it?’”

## 7. Proposed pilot — 2 minutes

Do not invent a final price in the first conversation if pricing has not been approved.

Offer one of these structures after needs analysis:

### Option A — Licensed Hanen programme pilot
Use only if the team/population matches the official programme and CJ has completed programme refresh/readiness.

### Option B — APC focused educator workshop + implementation follow-up
For autism-specific implementation problems.

Potential pilot structure:
1. short pre-training needs questionnaire,
2. live professional-development session,
3. participant action plan,
4. 3–4 week implementation check-in,
5. short leadership summary of implementation themes.

No guaranteed learner outcomes.

## 8. Questions to ask the buyer

- What would make this training worth paying for?
- What would you want educators doing differently four weeks later?
- Have you paid for external professional development before?
- What worked or did not work about it?
- Do you require HRD Corp claimability?
- Does your organisation need formal CPD documentation?
- Would leadership prefer one whole-team cohort or optional individual enrolment?
- Who else needs to approve a pilot?

## 9. Safe answers to difficult questions

### “Are you a therapist?”
“No. My role through APC is educator/consultant. APC provides education, consultation and professional development, not speech-language therapy, diagnosis or psychological services.”

### “Will our staff become certified?”
“They will be completing professional development. Attendance does not make participants Hanen-certified providers or trainers. Participant documentation follows the relevant programme requirements.”

### “Can you guarantee our students improve?”
“No. I do not guarantee child outcomes. The immediate professional-development outcomes I can responsibly focus on are educator learning, implementation and review of practice.”

### “Is this HRD Corp claimable?”
“Not currently. I am tracking whether organisations require that before deciding whether to enter the HRD Corp route.”

### “Why should we choose this over a cheaper workshop?”
“I would compare the offers based on fit rather than certificate count. My approach is to clarify the practice problem, use a structured programme where appropriate, and build in application and review rather than treating attendance as the endpoint.”

## 10. Evidence appendix for the meeting

Keep these available, not necessarily on every slide:

- Tan & Abdullah (2024), Malaysian mainstream preschool teachers and ASD knowledge/attitudes/self-efficacy.
- Yee et al. (2025), Klang Valley preschool teacher ASD knowledge.
- KPM special-education policy priorities.
- Kraft, Blazar & Hogan (2018), teacher coaching meta-analysis.
- Hanen LLLI research summary and relevant peer-reviewed papers.

## 11. What not to show in a sales presentation

- Hanen member-only programme slides.
- Hanen embedded training videos.
- proprietary Hanen handouts.
- identifiable child/client information.
- SCERTS proprietary materials unless use is explicitly authorised.
- claims of government accreditation / HRD claimability not actually held.

## 12. Close

“Based on what you have described, I can send you a short written recommendation for the most appropriate next step rather than forcing your team into a programme that does not fit. If there is a good match, the proposal will specify the audience, format, training hours, implementation follow-up and what we will measure.”
