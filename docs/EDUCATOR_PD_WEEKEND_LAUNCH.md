## Post 2 — problem awareness

### Hook

**One autism workshop does not automatically change what happens on Monday morning.**

### Caption

Professional learning matters, but information alone is not the same as implementation.

A teacher may understand regulation, prompting or communication in theory and still need help deciding what to do during a transition, group activity or difficult classroom moment.

That is why APC's school support is being built around a simple sequence:

**identify → learn → practise → implement → review**

For some teams, the right next step may be a structured professional-development programme. For others, it may be a focused consultation around one recurring problem.

The starting question is not, “Which course do you want?”

It is: **What is happening now that you would like your team to handle better?**

School and educator enquiries are now open through the APC website.

## Post 3 — Hanen clarity

### Hook

**Not every programme I offer schools is an autism programme. That distinction matters.**

### Caption

I am certified/licensed to provide several Hanen early-childhood educator programmes, including Learning Language and Loving It™, the Teacher Talk™ Training Series and ABC and Beyond™.

These programmes focus on areas such as educator-child interaction, early language, social development and emergent literacy. They are designed for early childhood settings and are **not autism-specific programmes**.

My autism-specific school work is separate and draws on my background in special and inclusive education, long-term school-based experience and additional professional learning.

Keeping those two areas separate helps me stay clear about what each programme is designed to do and what it is not designed to do.

More information is available at autismpathwaysconsulting.com/schools.

## Post 4 — buyer-facing leadership post

### Hook

**If every educator is supporting the same child differently, the issue may not be a lack of caring. It may be an implementation problem.**

### Caption

School teams often have good intentions and plenty of advice available to them.

The harder questions are usually practical:

What are we actually trying to change?
What should staff do consistently?
What happens when the strategy does not work as expected?
How do we know whether training changed practice?

APC's school and educator support is being developed around those questions.

For leadership teams, the first step is a short needs conversation rather than automatically choosing a course.

School, centre and educator enquiries are now open.
