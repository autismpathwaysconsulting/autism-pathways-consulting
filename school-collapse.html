Fine at school, falls apart at home

By CJ LimAutism Educator & Founder of APC

Reviewed 17 September 2026

The teacher describes a good day. At home, your child cries, refuses familiar activities or goes very quiet. Those two accounts can both be accurate. What happens after school deserves attention even when difficulties are not obvious in class.

In this guide
  • A calm school day may still take effort
  • Plan a gentler arrival
  • Try, notice and adjust
  • When to seek more support

A calm school day may still take effort

Lessons, noise, social expectations and transitions can be demanding. Some children hold back distress at school; others may be tired, hungry, uncomfortable or anxious about what comes next. “After-school restraint collapse” is a descriptive phrase, not a diagnosis. It does not prove that masking, or feeling safe at home, explains your child's response.

Compare what happens across the day before settling on one explanation. Include your child's perspective and consider both school and home demands.

Plan a gentler arrival

1

Start with immediate needs

Have an accepted snack and drink available, and make it easy to use the toilet or change uncomfortable clothes. Offer help without turning arrival into a series of questions.

2

Leave room to recover

Try a short period without homework or non-essential chores. The right length varies. A quiet room may suit one child; movement, company or a familiar activity may suit another. Ask or observe what they prefer.

3

Make the next step clear

When your child is ready, show what comes next in a form they understand. For example: “Snack, then a rest. We will look at homework together afterwards.” Adjust the plan if they are still struggling.

4

Talk with the school

Share a specific observation: “On three days this week, they cried for about half an hour after pickup.” Ask about breaks, transitions, eating, peer interactions and requests for help. Describe concerns without assuming either setting is at fault.

A message you can adapt for a teacher

“We are seeing a difficult period after pickup. Could we compare notes about the last part of the school day, any changes and how they ask for a break? We would like to agree one adjustment and review what happens.” If your child reports bullying or feeling unsafe, raise that directly through the school's safeguarding process.

Try, notice and adjust

For a week, note arrival time, food and rest, the day's events and how your child communicates or recovers. Try one change to the arrival routine. Review whether it made the evening more manageable, and whether distress also needs addressing during the school day.

When to seek more support

Persistent distress, school avoidance, pain, major sleep changes or a sudden change in behaviour warrant a conversation with the school and an appropriate health professional. A home routine alone may not address the cause.
