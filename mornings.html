When mornings are chaos

By CJ LimAutism Educator & Founder of APC

Reviewed 17 September 2026

The bag is open, breakfast is unfinished and you have already asked about shoes three times. When the clock is moving faster than your child, another reminder can feel like the only option. A routine that shows the next step may give you something else to try.

In this guide
  • Find the point where the morning gets stuck
  • Build a routine your child can use
  • Try, notice and adjust
  • When to seek more support

Find the point where the morning gets stuck

Getting ready involves remembering, switching activities, managing sensations and understanding time. Difficulty could also involve poor sleep, discomfort, worry about school or a task your child does not yet manage independently. Slowness alone does not tell you why they are stuck.

Make the routine easier to understand, then check whether your child can actually do the steps with the support available.

Build a routine your child can use

1

Choose a short, realistic sequence

Start with the part that causes most difficulty, rather than a chart of every action. For example: breakfast, teeth, clothes, bag. Break a step down further only if that helps your child.

2

Choose a format they understand

Use words, photographs, symbols or objects. A picture is not automatically meaningful to every child. Show what it refers to, and put the reminder where the activity happens rather than on a distant wall.

3

Use it together first

Model checking the next step. Point or give a brief prompt if needed; “What is next?” can itself be another demand. Offer help with difficult tasks. Independent use may take time, and some children will continue to need support.

4

Remove a practical obstacle

Prepare a comfortable outfit, put the bag in an agreed place or check what is needed the evening before. Choose something an adult can reliably maintain too. The system should reduce work, not become another daily argument.

When the plan changes

Use a simple “change” note or explain the different step in your child's usual communication format. On a rushed or difficult morning, prioritise essentials and provide more help. A missed step does not undo learning. If dressing repeatedly causes distress, investigate fit, seams, temperature and other possible discomfort rather than adding more reminders.

Try, notice and adjust

Choose one difficult part of the morning and try the visual support there. Notice whether your child understands it, where they still need help and what changes when you offer that help. Review the format with them. There is no two-week deadline for a routine to work.

When to seek more support

If mornings involve persistent pain, severe distress, sleep problems or fear of school, discuss the concern with the relevant health professional or school team. A checklist cannot resolve an unmet health, learning or safety need.
